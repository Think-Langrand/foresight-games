# Playing Solo — A Quick Guide

*Build a small future of public health, one card at a time. Takes about 10–15
minutes. No sign-up, nothing to lose your place on — your worlds are saved right
on this device.*

---

## The idea in one breath

Public health in 2035 hinges on a handful of genuinely open questions — Will
people still act on health collectively? Who will they trust? Who owns the data
coming off our bodies? Each of those questions can resolve more than one way.
You're going to pick **three** of them, choose **how each one lands**, and then
describe the world those three choices add up to.

That's the whole game: **three cards, one world.**

---

## Walkthrough

### 1. Start a world
On **Your Worlds**, tap **+ Build a new world**. You'll land on the builder with
three slots to fill and **slot one already pre-selected** to get you going.

### 2. Fill the three slots
Each slot is one **uncertainty** (an open question) plus one **outcome** (the way
you've decided it resolves).

- **Slot 1** comes pre-selected. Keep it, or tap **Unlock to change** and pick
  your own — it's a free choice like the others.
- **Slots 2 and 3** — tap to open the board, choose an uncertainty from a
  different area, then choose one of its four outcomes.

Each of your three cards must come from a **different area**, so your world
touches three different parts of the system rather than three flavors of the same
thing.

> **A note on the outcomes.** Most are plausible "Core" futures. A few are tagged
> **Edge** (the darker, complicating version) or **Wildcard** (a genuine shock).
> You don't have to pick a gloomy one — but one Edge card often makes a world far
> more interesting to write about.

### 3. Describe the world
Once all three are set, a short set of prompts opens up. Answer them in any order
— you can jump around and come back:

- **Defining characteristics** — two or three things true across this whole world.
- **Central theme** — the through-line, if there is one.
- **The new normal** — what feels ordinary here that would feel strange today.
- **Broken assumption** — what we believe about public health that no longer holds.
- **In one sentence** — fill in the blanks:
  *Because ___ has developed this way, ___ becomes common, which enables ___,
  which ultimately changes ___ for public health.*
- **Name your world** — a short, evocative title.

Everything autosaves as you type.

### 4. Submit
When it feels finished, submit. Your world joins the **shared gallery** with
everyone else's, and it stays in **Your Worlds** on this device so you can build
another.

## How to choose well

The good scenarios come from a specific kind of choosing. Aim for both of these
at once:

**Pick cards that feel like they belong together.** Your three outcomes should
read as one coherent world, not three unrelated headlines. If trust has moved to
platforms *and* private companies see more health data than agencies do *and*
care has drifted into an unregulated shadow system — that's a world with an
internal logic. It hangs together.

**But choose so they pull against each other.** The best worlds aren't tidy.
Look for the friction:

- A hopeful outcome sitting next to a hollowed-out one — *strong public trust,
  but no institutional base to act on it.* Now what?
- Two "good" outcomes that quietly undercut each other — *powerful new AI tools,
  owned entirely by vendors the public can't govern.*
- A world that's clearly better for some people and worse for others.

If your three cards all point the same direction, you've built a mood, not a
scenario. If they point in directions that create a real bind, you've got
something worth describing — and the four prompts above practically write
themselves. **Coherent enough to be a world; tense enough to be a question.**

## Good to know

- **No account, no code.** Solo worlds live on this device. Clearing your browser
  storage forgets them (but submitted worlds stay in the shared gallery).
- **Drafts are fine.** Leave any time via **← My worlds**; a half-built world
  waits for you.
- **Build as many as you like.** A second world exploring the *opposite* choices
  is one of the most useful things you can do.
