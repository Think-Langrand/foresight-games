# DG4 co-lead guide: the build spec

**For:** the agent building `NNPHI_DG4_CoLead_Guide`. Use the `langrand-colead-deck` skill: build from a spec with `scripts/langrand.js`, read `references/voice.md` before writing any copy, and keep the house style. The DG3 v5 deck is the template for structure; this document is the content.

**Why this document exists:** Session 4 is built in the portal. Every prompt, button and counter below is read off the live board, not proposed. Where a session fact is not yet decided it is marked **CONFIRM** rather than invented.

**The one hard rule you will break by accident:** no em-dashes, anywhere. Session 4's board copy has none; keep it that way.

---

## What changed, if you have an earlier version of this spec

**Session 4 was restructured.** Two of the five steps are gone and two are new. If you built anything from the previous spec, the method sections and all five breakouts change; the session furniture (cover, how the meetings fit, prep checklist, index, closing) survives.

| # | Change | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The handoff is no longer a step.** The whole week builds it, so the separate "assemble the handoff" breakout is gone. Step 5 is now a review of the week, and a Submit button. | Section 1 |
| 2 | **Backcasting is cut.** No timeline, no milestones, no 2027 to 2036 span, no five-period grid. Do not teach it and do not put it on the agenda. | Everywhere |
| 3 | **The three-move down-select is cut.** The group no longer ticks three cards off the response wall. It WRITES three bold moves, in a prompt, with the wall beside it as raw material. | Section 1, step 4 |
| 4 | **New step 1: key changes and assumptions.** Two prompts. Replaces the aspiration. | Section 1 |
| 5 | **New step 2: risks and opportunities on the impact x plausibility matrix.** Session 3's risks and opportunities, placed and scored on the same two 1 to 5 scales the group used in Session 2. This is the biggest new thing to teach. | Section 1, section 3 |
| 6 | **The aspiration is now the role.** One prompt on step 4: what role is public health playing in this future. | Section 1, step 4 |
| 7 | **A floor, not a cap.** Step 2 asks for at least 5 risks and at least 5 opportunities, with no ceiling. Every cap language from the old spec ("up to three", "the unpicked go dim") is wrong now. | Section 1 |

## 0. What was promised, and where each piece comes from

NNPHI was told Session 4 produces a handoff combining outputs from previous sessions:
"how the world works, its biggest shifts from today, top three risks, top three
opportunities, an ideal vision for public health in this future, possible strategic
directions, and unresolved uncertainties."

Six of the seven are covered. The seventh is a gap, flagged rather than papered over.

| Promised | Where it comes from | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| How the world works | Session 1: "What feels most important or distinctive?" and "Stepping into the future, what's different" | Handoff deck, slide 02 |
| Its biggest shifts from today | The six key changes, plus Session 4 step 1's own reading | Deck slides 03 and 10 |
| Top three risks | Session 3's risks, placed and scored on step 2's matrix | Deck slide 08 |
| Top three opportunities | Session 3's opportunities, same | Deck slide 08 |
| An ideal vision for public health | The role, Session 4 step 4 | Deck slide 11 |
| Possible strategic directions | The response wall and the three bold moves | Deck slide 12 |
| Unresolved uncertainties | **Nowhere, currently.** See below. | **Missing** |

**The uncertainties gap, stated plainly.** Session 1 asked for uncertainties directly, in
three questions, and every group answered. Those answers are still in the portal and still
in the facilitator's CSV. What changed is that the old step 5 asked the group which were
still open, and the handoff deck had a slide for them. Both are now off: the review was
dropped with the handoff step, and the deck slide is behind a flag.

Two ways to close it, both cheap, and **Tom decides which** (CONFIRM 1):

1. **Turn the deck slide back on.** Every Session 1 uncertainty goes to the committee as
   written. No group effort, no session time, and the honest default: nothing was answered
   unless someone says so.
2. **Add a sixth prompt to step 1**, asking what the group still cannot answer. Costs
   session time and gives a shorter, sharper list in the group's current words.

Until Tom chooses, **do not write a slide or a SAY line that promises the committee a list
of unresolved uncertainties.** If it ships as option 1 the deck gains a slide and no
breakout changes; if option 2, step 1 gains a prompt and slide 10 covers it.

**Note on where "top three" went.** The promise says top three risks and top three
opportunities. The board asks for at least five of each, ranked. That OVER-delivers rather
than under: the committee can read the top three off a ranked list of eight, and a list of
eight with scores shows how far apart the group put them. Slide 08 leads with the highest.
Say "your highest-ranked risks and opportunities", never "your top three".

## 1. What the app actually does (the target)

Session 4 is one shared board per group with a step row at the top, the same shape as
Session 3. Five steps, free navigation, all open from the first paint. The deck needs
**five breakouts that match them one for one.**

| Step | Tab label in the app | What the group produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1 · Key changes** | Two prompts, each taking as many short answers as the group has. |
| 2 | **2 · Risks & opportunities** | Session 3's risks and opportunities placed on a matrix and scored on two 1 to 5 scales. |
| 3 | **3 · Strategic responses** | A wall of cards, one move per card. |
| 4 | **4 · Role & bold moves** | Two prompts: the role, then three bold moves. |
| 5 | **5 · Review & submit** | The whole week read back, a preview of the handoff deck, and a Submit button. |

### The exact on-screen text to mirror

**Step 1, two prompts** (each takes several answers, one card each):

| Prompt | Hint under it |
|---|---|
| Summarize the key changes in this world. | What is different here, said in a few lines someone could read without the scenario in front of them. |
| What do we assume about how public health works today that does not hold here? | Not what breaks. What we take for granted now that this world quietly stops being true. |

Beside the prompts, a read-only rail headed **"What you said in Session 3"** with the line
"Your own words, for reference. Nothing here changes." It lists every hope and fear with
each flipped hope indented under the fear it came from, then folds for **Risks**,
**Opportunities** and **Themes**, each showing its count when shut. Nothing in it is
editable and nothing was copied onto this week's board.

**Step 2, the matrix.** Two panels down the left, coral **Risks** and lime
**Opportunities**, each listing every Session 3 card grouped under the theme it was written
on. A sticky matrix sits on the right.

- Each row has a **Place** button. Placing reveals two score rows and a **Remove** button.
- The two scales are **Plausibility** (`Far-fetched` to `Near certain`) and **Impact**
  (`Marginal` to `Transformative`), each 1 to 5, the same control Session 2 used on the key
  changes.
- Each panel header counts `{n} of 5 placed`, then either `{total} to choose from` or, once
  the floor is met, `place more if they matter · {total} in all`.
- A theme with nothing placed from it is called out in coral: `nothing placed from this
  theme`.
- The matrix plots only placed cards that have BOTH scores. Chips are coral for risks and
  lime for opportunities, numbered by rank, with a legend under it.

Directions panel, "Placing what is at stake":

- Place at least 5 risks and 5 opportunities. Place more if they matter; there is no ceiling.
- Plausibility is not the same as nearness. Ask whether this is already happening, or whether it needs several other things to happen first.
- Impact is how much changes if it lands, not how much you dislike it.
- Score as a group, with one person recording. Two people scoring the same card race each other.

**Step 3, the response wall.** Blue wall titled "Strategic responses". The add slot asks
"What could we do that moves us towards this?" and the composer's placeholder is "One move,
said as something someone does…". Empty state: "What would it take to get there?". Add
button reads "＋ Add a response". **No stars and no counter on this wall** (see step 4).
Directions, "Opening the option space":

- One move per card. Say what someone would do, not what should be true.
- Volume first. The next step asks for three bold moves and this wall is what you build them from, so give yourselves material.
- Aim for more than 3. A wall of three leaves you nothing to combine.
- A response can be uncomfortable. Nothing here commits you to it.

**Step 4, the role and the moves.** Two prompts, same shape as step 1:

| Prompt | Hint under it |
|---|---|
| What role is public health playing in this future? | The role you would argue for, not the one you expect. Say it as something already true in this world. |
| What three bold moves would you make to get there? | One move per answer. Something someone does, with your group in the room when it happens. |

Below them, the response wall again, **read-only**, headed "Your responses" with a count and
an "Edit on step 3" link. Its microcopy is the line the deck has to carry:

> These are yours from step 3. You are not picking from them. Look for two that are stronger together, or one that makes another possible.

**Step 5, review and submit.** Three blocks:

1. **Your handoff.** A link reading `Preview →` with "opens a new tab", above the line
   "Four sessions on one page, the way the steering committee will meet it."
2. **The submit bar.** Before: heading "Finished?", body "Submitting tells the facilitators
   this week is done. It does not lock the board and it is not final, so you can still
   change your mind.", button **Submit this week**. After: "Submitted {date} by {name}",
   body "The facilitators can see you are finished. The board is still open, so you can keep
   editing.", with **Undo**.
3. **The week, read back.** The whole week in step order: the step 1 answers, the matrix as
   a ranked list with each score printed, the response wall, then the role and the moves.
   Nothing on it is editable.

Directions, "Reading it back":

- Read it as the steering committee will: top to bottom, with none of this session in your head.
- A gap you can see here is a gap they will see. Go back to the step and fill it.
- Submitting tells the facilitators you are finished. It does not lock anything, so you can still fix a line afterwards.

Step 1's answers are pinned read-only at the top of step 3, headed "What changes in this
world", with an "Edit on step 1" link.

### The floor, and the honest wording for it

- **At least 5 risks and 5 opportunities placed. No ceiling, and nothing is enforced.** The
  counter reads `3 of 5 placed` and keeps counting past five. Groups wrote between 14 and 44
  risks plus opportunities, so a cap tuned for one group would starve another. Say "at least
  five of each"; never "up to", never "the portal will stop you".
- **Three bold moves is the prompt's wording, not a limit.** It is a free-text prompt that
  takes any number of answers. A group that writes two, or four, gets no complaint from the
  software. Say "three" because that is what is asked for, and let co-leads accept two they
  mean.
- **Submit is not a lock.** Any member can submit, any member can undo, and the board stays
  editable afterwards. Never imply the group is shut out after pressing it.

### What Session 4 does NOT have, so the deck must not promise it

- No backcasting, no timeline, no milestones, no dates between 2027 and 2036.
- No down-select on the response wall. Nothing on step 3 is starred, ticked or chosen.
- No seeding or re-entry of Session 3 material. It is read-only reference beside the work,
  except on step 2, where the group places Session 3's own cards without retyping them.
- No new risks or opportunities on step 2. Only Session 3's, so there is one source of truth.
- No tagging a response to a theme, risk or opportunity. Responses are free-standing.
- No resolution of the Session 1 uncertainties, and currently no review of them at all.
- No revisiting the role of public health in Session 3's sense. Step 4 asks it fresh.

Totals 120. Lime bands on rows 03, 05, 08, 10, 11. Side notes: keep "Insights and answers
are saved in the portal" under BEFORE EVERY RETURN TO THE MAIN ROOM; add a HOW THE SESSION
RUNS note reading "Langrand teaches each method, then you lead five breakouts. Two

Breakout 2 is the longest because it is the only step that asks the group to score anything,
and scoring as a group is slow by design. Breakout 5 is the shortest because every word it
touches is already written: it is a reading exercise, not a writing one.

The first share-out sits after breakout 2 rather than after breakout 1, because step 1 is a
summary and step 2 is a judgement. "What did you rank highest and why" is a share-out worth
eight minutes; "read us your summary" is not.

### Progress bar (every method and breakout slide), 8 phases

`01 Welcome · 02 The world · 03 Breakout 1 · 04 The matrix · 05 Breakout 2 · 06 Responses + moves · 07 Review · 08 Share-out + close`

Keep each label under 26 characters. Do not put every agenda row on the bar.

### Outputs (slide 06)

1. **A plain reading of this world**, and the assumptions about public health today that it
   quietly ends. Several answers per prompt is normal and good.
2. **Your risks and opportunities ranked**, at least five of each, scored on impact against
   plausibility.
3. **A wall of strategic responses**, one move per card. More than three, so you have
   something to build from.
4. **The role public health plays here**, and three bold moves to get there.
5. **A handoff deck, reviewed and submitted.** Twelve slides drawn from all four sessions.

Keep the "Write for people who weren't in the room" card. Replace any "All of it carries
forward" card with a `GOOD TO KNOW` reading "Nothing is written twice. Every step today
either reads earlier work back or adds the one thing only your group can say."

### 02 · The method: reading the world back

**09 · Divider.** Num 2, eyebrow `THE METHOD · PART 02`, title "What this world changes", sub "Your group's own summary of the scenario, and the assumptions it quietly ends."

**10 · Two questions, and the second is the one that earns its keep.** `definitionPair`:

- `IT IS`: A plain summary a reader could follow without the scenario in front of them, and then the harder half: which of today's working assumptions stop being true here.
- `IT IS NOT`: A list of what breaks. "Funding collapses" is a risk and Session 3 has those. An assumption is something nobody argues about today because everyone takes it for granted.

Then a `bluePanel` worked example, labelled as hypothetical:

> `EXAMPLE · AN ASSUMPTION THAT STOPS HOLDING`
> "We assume the evidence decides. In this world residents can overrule a scored option, and the question stops being what the data says and becomes who gets to choose. Nothing in how we are set up today answers that."

`SAY · TO OPEN`: "Two questions. The first is a summary, and you will get through it fast. The second is the one I want your twelve minutes on: what do we take for granted today that this world stops being true?"

**11 · Why this step comes first.** `labeledText` plus one `card`:

> The committee will read your ranking and your three moves. Neither makes sense without this. A move that is bold today may be ordinary here, and a risk that keeps you up at night may be solved in this world. This is the step that tells a reader which one they are looking at.

`WATCH FOR` card: "A group that writes the scenario's own words back. Ask them what a colleague who missed every session would need to be told."

### 03 · Breakout 1

### 04 · The method: impact and plausibility

**15 · Divider.** Num 3, eyebrow `THE METHOD · PART 03`, title "What matters most", sub "Your risks and opportunities, ranked against each other for the first time."

- `PLAUSIBILITY`: 1 is far-fetched, 5 is near certain. Not the same as nearness. Ask whether this is already happening, or whether several other things have to happen first.
- `IMPACT`: 1 is marginal, 5 is transformative. How much changes if it lands. Not how much you dislike it.

`card` with `tag: do`: "Score the pair together and move on. The ranking comes from the two numbers multiplied, so a card nobody can agree on is usually a 3 and 3, and that is a real answer."

**17 · The floor, and why there is no ceiling.** `labeledText`:

`SAY · TO OPEN`: "Session 3 gave everything on your board the same weight. Nothing on it says which risk you would actually act on first. That is what the next eighteen minutes are for."

### 06 · The method: strategic responses

### 08 · The method: the role, and three bold moves

**27 · Divider.** Num 5, eyebrow `THE METHOD · PART 05`, title "The role, and the moves", sub "The one argument in four sessions, and the three things your group would do about it."

**28 · The role is an argument, not an observation.** `definitionPair`:

- `IT IS`: The role you would argue for, said as something already true in this world. "Public health is the convenor that holds the evidence while residents decide."
- `IT IS NOT`: The role you expect, or the one you have today. This is the one place in four sessions where your group says what public health is for.

`card` with `tag: say`: "Everything else today was a reading of what is there. This is the only question where your group's answer is its own."

**29 · Three bold moves, written rather than picked.** Explain the shape explicitly, because the old session worked differently and some co-leads will expect a checklist:

> Your response wall sits beside this prompt, read-only. You are not ticking three of it. A bold move can be two responses put together, or one response made possible by another, or something the wall made you think of that is not on it.

Quote the board's own microcopy as a `card`:

> "These are yours from step 3. You are not picking from them. Look for two that are stronger together, or one that makes another possible."
