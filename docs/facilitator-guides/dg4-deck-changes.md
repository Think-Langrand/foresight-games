**For:** the agent updating `NNPHI_DG4_CoLead_Guide`. You have already built a 39-slide deck from the first spec. This document is the delta against **that deck**, slide by slide, so you can revise rather than rebuild. The full target is `dg4-deck-spec.md`, which has been rewritten for the new week; where the two disagree, the spec wins.

## 1. The old week and the new week

| | v1 deck teaches | The board now does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aspiration (3 prompts) | **Key changes** (2 prompts) |
| 2 | Strategic responses | **Risks & opportunities** on a matrix |
| 3 | Bold moves (tick 3 off the wall) | **Strategic responses** |
| 4 | Backcasting (5 periods, 2027 to 2036) | **Role & bold moves** (2 prompts) |
| 5 | The handoff (star 3+3, review uncertainties, one line) | **Review & submit** |

Steps 2 and 3 **swapped places and changed meaning**. Step 3 in v1 was a down-select; step 3 now is the response wall that used to be step 2. If you reuse a slide, check which step it is teaching before you move it.

### The five things that are gone

Delete every trace of these. They are not "deprioritised", they are not on the board:

1. **Backcasting.** No timeline, no milestones, no five two-year periods, no 2027 to 2036 span, no "it is 2036 and this has happened".
2. **The three-move down-select.** Nothing on the response wall is ticked, starred or chosen. No "at three the other tick boxes go dim". No "4 of 3 chosen".
3. **Starring three risks and three opportunities.** Replaced by scoring, with a floor of five per kind and no ceiling.
4. **The Session 1 uncertainties review.** See the open question in section 7.
5. **The one line for the committee.** No such prompt exists.

### The two new things to teach

1. **The impact × plausibility matrix** (step 2). Session 3's risks and opportunities, scored on two 1-to-5 scales. This is the only step in Session 4 that asks a group to score anything, and it is the longest breakout. The group has used these exact scales before, on the Session 2 key changes, so lead with that.
2. **A floor, not a cap.** Every "up to three" in v1 becomes "at least five, more if they matter". This inverts the facilitation: v1 asked co-leads to hold a group *back*, this asks them to keep a group *going*.

**Sums to 120.** Lime bands on 03, 05, 08, 10, 11. **CONFIRM the split with Tom.**

Pacing note for the side panel: *"Breakout 2 is the longest, because it is the only step that asks the group to score anything and scoring as a group is slow by design. Breakout 5 is the shortest, because every word it touches is already written."*

The first share-out moved to **after breakout 2**, on purpose: step 1 is a summary and step 2 is a judgement, and only one of those is worth eight minutes of the room's time.

### The five breakouts, run-it timings

**Breakout 1 · Key changes and assumptions (12 min).** 2 restate the task · 4 the first prompt, three or four short lines · 5 the second prompt, let it be slow · 1 check both have something in them.

**Breakout 2 · Risks and opportunities (18 min).** 2 agree who records, only one person touches the scores · 3 read the risks top to bottom without scoring · 6 score the risks, say both numbers aloud before the recorder clicks · 6 the same for opportunities · 1 look at the themes flagged with nothing scored from them.

**Breakout 3 · Strategic responses (14 min).** 1 restate: moves, not conditions · 4 everyone adds, nobody comments · 6 walk the matrix and ask what you would do about the top three · 3 reread and sharpen any card a reader would not follow.

**Breakout 4 · The role and three bold moves (15 min).** 1 the role first, do not let the moves start early · 5 the role, push for one sentence the whole group would defend · 7 three bold moves, one answer each, reading the response wall beside the prompt · 2 read them back and merge any two that are the same move.

**Breakout 5 · Review and submit (8 min).** 1 open the preview, it opens in a new tab · 4 read it as the committee will · 2 fix one thing, on the step that owns it · 1 press Submit.

### Outputs (slide 06)

1. **A plain reading of this world,** and the assumptions about public health today that it quietly ends.
2. **Your risks and opportunities ranked,** at least five of each, scored on impact against plausibility.
3. **A wall of strategic responses,** one move per card.
4. **The aspirational role for public health here,** and the boldest moves to reach it.
5. **A handoff deck, reviewed and submitted.** Twelve slides drawn from all four sessions.

Keep the "Write for people who weren't in the room" card. Replace the `GOOD TO KNOW` with: *"Nothing today is capped. Step 2 asks for at least five of each, and the only limit anywhere is the three bold moves, which is what we ask for rather than what the software enforces."*

### The right rail, on steps 1 and 3

### Step 2, the matrix

Two panels down the left, coral **Risks** and lime **Opportunities**, grouped under the Session 3 theme each card was written on. **Every card shows both scales immediately.** There is no "place this one" button, and the group may well ask for one. Scoring both scales is what puts a card on the matrix.

- Scales: **Plausibility** (`Far-fetched` → `Near certain`) and **Impact** (`Marginal` → `Transformative`), 1 to 5.
- Panel header: `{n} of {total} scored`, then `at least 5`, or once past it `past 5 · keep going if they matter`.
- A theme with nothing scored from it is flagged in coral: `nothing scored from this theme`.
- A card with one scale set says `Both scores put it on the matrix.`
- A scored card offers `Clear`.
- Right: the matrix, chips coral for risks and lime for opportunities, numbered by rank, with a legend and a progress bar reading `{n} of {total} scored · both scales set`.

Directions, **"Ranking what is at stake"**:

- Score at least 5 risks and 5 opportunities. Score more if they matter; there is no ceiling.
- A card reaches the matrix when both of its scales are set. Leave the ones you have nothing to say about alone.
- Plausibility is not the same as nearness. Ask whether this is already happening, or whether it needs several other things to happen first.
- Impact is how much changes if it lands, not how much you dislike it.
- Score as a group, with one person recording. Two people scoring the same card race each other.

### Step 3, the response wall

Blue wall titled **Strategic responses**. Add slot: *"What could we do that moves us towards this?"*. Composer: *"One move, said as something someone does…"*. Empty state: *"What would it take to get there?"*. Button: `＋ Add a response`. **No stars and no counter.**

Directions, **"Opening the option space"**:

- One move per card. Say what someone would do, not what should be true.
- Volume first. The next step asks for three bold moves and this wall is what you build them from, so give yourselves material.
- Aim for more than 3. A wall of three leaves you nothing to combine.
- A response can be uncomfortable. Nothing here commits you to it.

### Step 4, the role and the moves

| Prompt | Hint under it |
|---|---|
| What is the aspirational role for public health in this world? | What the field is reaching for here, not what you expect it to settle for. Say it as something already true in this world. The moves below are how you get to it. |
| What are the boldest moves that can be made in public health in this scenario to help you meet your aspirations? | Three of them, one per answer. Something someone does, with your group in the room when it happens. |

Below them, the response wall again, **read-only**, headed **Strategic responses** with a count and an "Edit on step 3" link. Its microcopy is the line the deck has to carry, because co-leads who ran v1 will expect a checklist:

> These are yours from step 3. You are not picking from them. Look for two that are stronger together, or one that makes another possible.

### Step 5, review and submit

1. **Your handoff** · *"Four sessions on one page, the way the steering committee will meet it."* · `Preview →` · *"opens a new tab"*
2. **Finished?** · *"Submitting tells the facilitators this week is done. It does not lock the board and it is not final, so you can still change your mind."* · button **Submit this week**. Afterwards: *"Submitted {date} by {name}"*, *"The facilitators can see you are finished. The board is still open, so you can keep editing."*, and **Undo**.
3. **The week read back**, in step order, nothing editable.

Directions, **"Reading it back"**:

- Read it as the steering committee will: top to bottom, with none of this session in your head.
- A gap you can see here is a gap they will see. Go back to the step and fill it.
- Submitting tells the facilitators you are finished. It does not lock anything, so you can still fix a line afterwards.
